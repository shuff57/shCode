# Module 7.5: Project — Defender, Part B (enemies and waves, the procedural way)

**Quarter 2 · the second of three Defender parts · after §7.4 Defender, Part A**

## SLO Focus
- **SLO 3:** the module's artifact is a playable shooter with enemies, hp, score and waves.
- Reinforces **SLO 2** and **SLO 4**.

## Module Goal
Students turn Part A's one drone into a field of enemies with hp and worth, shoot them with every shot against every enemy, bring them in waves, and feel what it costs to keep one enemy's facts in three arrays. Everything is still functions, variables and arrays: **no classes, no groups, no `extends`.** The classes come back in 7.6, which rebuilds this same game.

## Pre-requisite snapshot
§7.4 complete: a ship clamped to the canvas, shots on a cooldown that are deleted and spliced, `overlaps()` on a pass-through drone, base hp, the `playing` gate, and a `startRound()` that R calls. From earlier: nested loops and `break` (2.4.25-2.4.27), parallel arrays and why they drift (7.2.15), the wave check `enemies.length === 0` (7.3.17).

## Post-requisite snapshot
Can keep related facts in parallel arrays and keep them in step, test every member of one array against every member of another with a nested loop that splices safely, run waves with a pause, and find a drift bug. Can say, with numbers from their own code, what that layout costs. Ready for 7.6, where the same game is rebuilt with classes.

## Note
**Not a book section.** The middle part of the Defender capstone (`codehs-review/CAPSTONE-SPEC.md`, section 5). It is built the procedural way **on purpose**: 7.5.1 says so in its first paragraph, and the mess shows up in the student's own code (three pushes in `spawnEnemy`, the same delete-and-three-splices in two places, a nested loop with a splice and a `break`, three arrays to empty in `startRound`). 7.5.8 plants the bug that layout invites, and 7.5.9's last question asks how many places change to give every enemy one more fact.

**9 lessons** (`7.5.1`-`7.5.9`) in folders `5-10-defender-*`. All 0 points, as in 5.10 and 5.13.

**Words on screen go in `drawTop()`**, the convention 5.11.1 set: the score, wave, base hp and Game over / press R are drawn there in every lab and in the 7.5.6 example, so a falling enemy can never cover them.

### The one idea this module is really about
Two arrays that meet. The new technique is the nested hit loop (7.5.3 in plain words, 7.5.4 read, 7.5.5 written), where both loops run backwards and a hit `break`s the inner one. The new lesson is what parallel arrays cost: every push and every splice has to happen to all three arrays at the same index, and 7.5.8 shows what one forgotten `splice` does to a game.
