# Module 1.7: Chapter 1 Individual Performance Assessment

**Quarter 1 · Week 4 · Thu Sep 03, 2026 · One meeting · Individual · ~50 minutes**

The individual half of the chapter's assessment, and the sibling of the Group
PA two days before it: same chapter, same week, same in-app shape, sat alone.
Five lessons, 16 items, 100 points, and no working program written at any
point — because Chapter 1 has not taught a conditional, and an assessment that
outruns the book is testing shCode rather than the course.

## Why this is not the Group PA again, alone

The PA (1.6) already proves a pair can produce. What a paired build cannot tell
you is which of the two produced it.

| | 1.6 Group PA (Sep 1) | 1.7 Test (Sep 3) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | pairs | individual |
| Length | ~85 min over three lessons | ~49 min over five |
| The verb | **build** one thing end to end | **read · diagnose · design** |
| Code written | a whole documented program | none |
| Flowchart | 25 min, drawn together, gates the build | 8 min, drawn alone, gates the pseudocode |
| Coverage | all five modules through one artifact | all five modules as independent items |
| The failure it catches | cannot turn a plan into a program | cannot read code, cannot name a bug, **was carried** |

Tracing and diagnosis are the two verbs the PA structurally cannot isolate, and
they are the ones that generalise: a student who can only write and never read
produces code they cannot debug.

recall questions dressed as traces. Reviewed and accepted, 2026-09-02.

## The ceiling this test may not exceed

**No conditionals, no loops, no functions, no arrays, no objects.** Every snippet
on this test is a straight sequence. Part D is a chart plus pseudocode, never
JavaScript, for the same reason the PA's program has a diamond that computes a
boolean instead of an `if`.

**Three flowchart shapes — oval, rectangle, diamond** (Appendix D §D.2). Book
exercise 1.5.11 asks for exactly three; the parallelogram is a shCode addition.
The editor offers it and a student who uses one is not penalised, but nothing on
this test may *require* it.

## Three forms, assigned automatically

Same room, same minute, different papers — with no cards to hand out and no
roster for a teacher to keep.

```
1.7.1 quiz ── variants: ["a","b","c"] ── hash(lessonId + student email)
                                              │
        ┌─────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────┐
        ▼                                     ▼                     ▼
   3 common concepts               3 common concepts       3 common concepts
   + 5 traces (form a)             + 5 traces (form b)     + 5 traces (form c)
        └──────── questions and options shuffled per student ───────┘
```

Eighteen questions are authored; each student is asked eight. The five traces
are the same five *shapes* on every form — a `const` reassignment, a coercion, a
`typeof` surprise, a sticky `NaN`, a template literal — with different names and
numbers, so the forms are genuinely equivalent and a neighbour's screen is no
help. `lib/quiz-variant.ts` does the assignment; `scripts/test-quiz.mjs` refuses
to build a quiz whose forms come out different lengths.

**The other three lessons are single-form, deliberately.** Part C's answer is
typed into a file rather than picked from a list, so copying a neighbour means
retyping their code more slowly than fixing your own. Parts D1 and D2 offer a
menu of three problems and the student picks one, the same way the PA does.

## Test mode

Both `QuizConfig` and `AiGraderConfig` take `summative: true`, added for this
test and inherited by the other seven chapter tests.

| | A module quiz | A chapter test (`summative`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attempts | unlimited | one; the paper locks on submit |
| Marking shown | per-question, right or wrong | none |
| Explanations shown | yes, on every question | none |
| Score shown | yes | no — the teacher hands it back |
| Completion | on passing | **on submitting** |

Completing on submission rather than on score is the load-bearing one. The
course is green-to-advance, so a score gate on Part A would lock a student out
of Parts B through D of their own exam over an answer nobody has marked yet.

Without this mode a quiz is a study tool wearing a test's clothes: it marks each
answer, explains why, and invites another go.

## Grading and the gradebook

shCode records the work; it does not compute the 100. `points` is 0 on all five
lessons and `passingScore` is 0, as everywhere else in the course. The teacher
reads the submissions from the teacher view and enters one score per student in
Aeries, against the 15% Individual Chapter Tests category.

The AI grader marks 1.7.2 and 1.7.5 against their rubrics and stores the result;
under `summative` the student never sees it. Treat the model's marks as a first
pass to review, not as the grade.

## No retake

**Decided 2026-09-02.** The score a student earns on the day is the score, and
the curriculum plan's common-format rule was changed to match. There is no Form
R, no alternate paper and no second sitting.

That puts the whole weight of fairness on the three same-room forms and on the
open-date lock, because a student who sees the paper early has no second chance
to be assessed honestly and neither does anyone else. Keep the module locked
until the period starts.

An absence is the case this leaves open. A student who is not in the room on the
day cannot sit a paper that does not reopen, so that is a teacher judgement each
time rather than a policy the app enforces.

## Teacher notes

- **Hand the PA checklist back as the study guide on Sep 1.** Every item type on
  this test has an analogue in the PA the student just built. That is deliberate
  and it is not a secret.
- **Proctor Part B.** In-app means a console is a tab away, and a "what does this
  print" question is checkable in about twenty seconds by anyone who opens one.
  Shuffling and three forms defeat the over-the-shoulder glance; they do not
  defeat a second tab. Circulate.
- **The open-date lock is client-side only** (`components/LessonAccessGate.tsx`).
  It stops the lesson rendering early; it does not stop a determined student
  reaching the lesson's data through the API. Good enough for a lock that is
  really a courtesy, and worth knowing before you rely on it.
- **Read Part D1 and D2 together.** A correct chart with botched pseudocode is a
  different failure from no chart at all, and the 10/20 split is what makes that
  visible. D2 is graded against the problem, not against D1 — a student who
  charts one approach and writes a better one loses nothing. Only a blank D1
  costs the 10.
