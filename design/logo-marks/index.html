Identity study · motion

Four marks for shCode

Four motion studies for the name. A, B and C type the wordmark straight onto the background; D came from the editor animation already in hand, where the name is finished by autocomplete. The letters are real outlines taken from Hack Bold, converted to paths, so the SVGs carry no font dependency.

Each is one SVG with CSS motion inside it: no JavaScript, no animation library, a still frame that holds on its own, and nothing moving when the reader has asked for reduced motion.

In the app today: a document with </> inside it. Option A is the one that keeps that recognition.
Every mark also stops on its own under prefers-reduced-motion.

The idea
The wordmark sits inside the brackets, so sh reads as an element and the whole name is a tag. The green block that sits between the brackets in the favicon is the same block that types the letters.
What moves
Both brackets draw in from their tips, 140 ms apart. The block pops, then steps right one character at a time as shCode types in behind it.
Loop
5 s. The mark assembles in the first second; the wordmark is typed by 1.5 s and stays put for the rest.
Still frame
Brackets whole, shCode complete, block resting after the final e.
At 16 px
The brackets close into a diamond and the block still reads in the middle. The bracket meaning is lost; the shape is not.

The idea

        
No separate mark in the wordmark: the type is the logo, and the only drawing is the caret. It is the most literal reading of the name, and the least decorated.

        
What moves

        
The caret arrives, advances one character at a time, and each letter appears at its slot. It blinks at the end of the word, then the line clears.

4.6 s. The word is complete after 0.9 s and holds for the rest.

The idea

        
The s is drawn by a pen dot before any type appears, then the wordmark types itself in behind it. Motion first, letters second, which is the order the course teaches them in.

        
What moves

        
The pen travels the curve with the stroke trailing behind it and pulses once where it lands. The caret then steps through shCode one letter at a time.

5.6 s. The draw takes 1.6 s, the typing half a second, and the rest is stillness.

The idea

        
Start from the editor animation already in hand: shCode lives on line 1 of a code window and is finished the way an editor finishes a name. You type sh, the rest arrives as a dimmed suggestion, a return key appears under the caret, and accepting it sets Code solid.

        
What moves

        
The window draws itself, then the caret types s and h in brand blue. Code ghosts in at 28% opacity, the badge slides up, and on accept the four letters snap to white, the caret jumps to the end and blinks. The chrome never moves after it is drawn.

        
Loop

        
6 s. The name is complete by 2.1 s and holds for the rest of the cycle.

        
Still frame

        
The window, shCode complete — sh blue, Code white — caret resting after the final e. The return badge is gone.

        
At 16 px

        
The window and the green caret survive; the ghost detail does not. It is the only option whose mark is also a picture of the app.

What I would ship

    
D, Suggest. It is the only option that shows the name being finished rather than typed, and the only mark that is also a picture of the app. It is the closest translation of the animation you brought, and the window gives the lockup somewhere to sit on a lesson header or a sign-in screen. The trade is arithmetic: the frame takes vertical space, so the word is smaller at the same height.

    
C, Sweep still has the strongest favicon, and A is still the conservative pick that keeps continuity with the icon in the app today. B is the purist version and the cheapest to build. If the editor is the identity, ship D and use the window mark alone in the tab strip.

    
The two colours do fixed jobs. Blue is the app’s #5baafd, and it is the mark, the capital C, and the typed sh in D. Green is the colour the app already uses to mean advance, and it is only ever on the caret.
