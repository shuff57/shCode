## Chapter 1 Group PA — Part 3 of 3: Demo

**Worth 25%.** Two things happen in this third: you write this up, and you run the
program for your teacher with the writeup open next to it.

**What you'll be assessed on here:**
- Testing your own program instead of hoping it works
- Saying where four of Chapter 1's five sections show up in what you built
- Both partners being able to explain the whole thing, not half of it each

### Write it together, submit it twice

One answer, agreed on by both of you, pasted in by each of you. If the two of you
would not give the same answer to a question, that is the question to talk about
before you write it down.

### Before you start writing

Run your program three times with three different sets of numbers, and write down
what came out each time. You need those numbers for Part 2 and you will not
remember them.

**At least one of the three has to come out on the other side of the comparison.**
Three test cases that all say "good deal" have tested exactly half of your program.
1.5.40 is where that idea comes from, and this is the first time it costs you marks.

### The live demo

When your teacher comes to the table, one of you runs the program and the other
narrates. Then swap, and the second one answers a question about a line the first
one wrote. That is the whole reason both of you have to understand all of it: you
do not get to divide the program in half and each defend your own end.

### The six parts

They are in the panel with the response box. Short answers throughout — a sentence
or two each, except the test cases, which are a table.

Nothing in them is new. Part 2 is test cases from 1.5.40. Part 3 is `console.log`
debugging from 1.5.41. Part 4 is the four framework activities from 1.1.6. Part 5 is
the paradigm vocabulary from 1.4.8 plus the types from 1.2.18 and 1.2.23. Part 6 is
the two comment lines already sitting in your `script.js`.

The one section nothing below asks about is **1.3, documentation and conventions** — not
because it goes unassessed, but because it already was, in the program itself: the header,
the pseudocode comments and the variable names you chose in 1.6.2.

If a part sends you back to a lesson, go back and read it. That is what it is for.

### The one that catches people

**Part 4, the design-before-code sentence.** "Because we were told to" is not an
answer. You have just spent an hour on the other answer: you settled an argument on
the diagram in two minutes that would have cost you twenty minutes of rewriting had
you found it in the code. Say that, in your own words, about your own argument.
