## Chapter 1 Individual PA — Part 4 of 5: Plan It

**10 points. One short plan, typed alone.**

- Pick **one** problem below. Part 5 writes the steps for the *same* one.
- Type the plan as pseudocode in the editor: the keywords from 1.5.19 (`START`, `INPUT`,
  `SET`, `IF`, `ELSE`, `END IF`, `PRINT`, `END`) and the two rules from 1.5.20.
- No JavaScript is needed, and none is rewarded. Each time you submit, the AI marks
  whether your plan answers the problem you picked. A neat plan for the wrong problem
  still scores nothing.
- **You get three tries.** Submit, read the feedback on whether the plan answers your
  problem, rewrite and submit again. Your best try counts. After your third try you can see
  how it is solved, once your teacher releases it.

| Rule | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| One instruction per line | A line doing two things gets split |
| Indent what is inside something else | The line under `IF` and the line under `ELSE` are indented; `END IF` is not |

### Pick one

**1. Backpack Check** — you know the number of textbooks, what one textbook
weighs, and the student's own weight.

- Work out: what the backpack weighs
- Compare against: **one tenth of the student's weight**

**2. Screen Time** — you know the minutes of screen time used across **seven
days**.

- Work out: the daily average
- Compare against: **120 minutes a day**

**3. Print Job** — you know how many pages the document is, at **8 cents a
page**.

- Work out: what the job costs
- Compare against: the **$5.00** left in the print account

### The shape of a finished plan

Start → take in the numbers → work out the one value → ask the comparison with an
`IF` → a different report under the `IF` and under the `ELSE` → End.

Open the **Flow Chart** above the editor for a plan of a different problem (a lemonade
stand), drawn so you can see the shape. It is read-only and is not one of the three.

### What is marked

| Points | What earns them |
| ---: | --- |
| 4 | Takes in every value your problem names, and has a line that says **how** the one value is worked out |
| 3 | An `IF` that compares that value with your problem's own limit |
| 3 | A different report under the `IF` and under the `ELSE`, matching the question, then `END` |

Exact wording does not matter, and neither do spelling or neat layout. A plan whose
`IF` and reports point opposite ways, or that works out something the problem did not ask
for, does not solve the problem, even when every piece is there.

### Before you submit

Walk one set of real numbers through your plan out loud, and then a second set that comes
out on the other side of the `IF`. Does each side say the right thing?
