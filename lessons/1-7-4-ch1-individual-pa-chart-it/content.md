## Chapter 1 Individual PA — Part 4 of 5: Outline It

**10 points. One short outline, typed alone.**

- Pick **one** problem below. Part 5 writes the full steps for the *same* one.
- Fill in the five labelled lines in the editor. Short sentences are fine. You do not need
  keywords or indentation here; those are marked in Part 5.
- No JavaScript is needed, and none is rewarded. Each time you submit, the AI marks
  whether your outline answers the problem you picked. A neat outline for the wrong problem
  still scores nothing.
- **You get three tries.** Submit, read the feedback on whether the outline answers your
  problem, rewrite and submit again. Your best try counts. After your third try you can see
  how it is solved, once your teacher releases it.

| Line | What goes on it |
| --- | --- |
| GET | Every number the problem hands you |
| WORK OUT | The one value, written so it says how |
| ASK | The question you ask about that value, against the problem's own limit |
| YES | What the program reports when the answer is yes |
| NO | A different report for when the answer is no |

### Pick one

**1. Backpack Check** — you know the number of textbooks, what one textbook
weighs, and the student's own weight.

- Work out: what the backpack weighs
- Compare against: **one tenth of the student's weight**

**2. Screen Time** — you know the minutes of screen time used across **seven
days**.

- Work out: the daily average
- Compare against: **120 minutes a day**

**3. Print Job** — you know how many pages the document is, at **8 cents a
page**.

- Work out: what the job costs
- Compare against: the **$5.00** left in the print account

### The shape of a finished outline

Take in the numbers → work out the one value → ask the question → a different report on
the yes side and on the no side.

Open the **Flow Chart** above the editor for an outline of a different problem (a lemonade
stand), drawn so you can see the shape. It is read-only and is not one of the three.

### What is marked

| Points | What earns them |
| ---: | --- |
| 4 | Takes in every value your problem names, and says **how** the one value is worked out |
| 3 | Asks a question that compares that value with your problem's own limit |
| 3 | A different report on the yes side and on the no side, matching the question |

Exact wording does not matter, and neither do spelling or neat layout. An outline whose
question and reports point opposite ways, or that works out something the problem did not
ask for, does not solve the problem, even when every piece is there.

### Before you submit

Walk one set of real numbers through your outline out loud, and then a second set that
comes out on the other side of the question. Does each side say the right thing?
